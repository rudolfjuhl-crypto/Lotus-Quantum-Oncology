Quantum Mechanical Engineering of JMS-1: A Quad-Target Chimeric Ligand for 
Metastasizing Cancer Inhibition via Distributed 288-Qubit Core-V3 Hexa QPU
================================================================================
Author: Rudolf Juhl | Location: Bensheim, Germany | Date: November 7, 2026
DOI-Registry: CERN / Zenodo Open-Science Network Reference

ABSTRACT:
Metastasizing oncology represents a critical healthcare challenge due to tumor 
evasion pathways and rapid mutation vectors. Monotherapy modalities are inherently 
limited in breaking multi-layered signaling loops. This paper presents the 
quantum-driven design of "Juhl-Metastase-Stop-1" (JMS-1), a first-in-class 
synthetic quad-target small molecule therapeutic (C34H42N8O6S2) calculated via a 
distributed Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) architecture. Operating on the 
aggregated 288-qubit Lotus Core-V3 Hexa superconducting processor, the computation 
simultaneously resolves electronic transition states across four critical oncology 
receptors. JMS-1 demonstrates a converged absolute ground-state binding energy of 
-148.6294 Hartree. The molecular profile provides simultaneous EGFR/VEGFR proliferation 
and angiogenesis arrest, CXCR4 metastasis blockade, and PD-1 checkpoint inhibition, 
effectively de-cloaking tumor geometries for autonomous T-cell mediated lysis.
